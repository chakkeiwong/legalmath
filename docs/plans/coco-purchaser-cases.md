# Two real CoCo purchaser-route cases

## Question, comparator and evidence contract

Can the same declared investor pass the US initial-distribution purchaser route
for Barclays PLC's February 2025 7.625% CoCo and fail the route for Standard
Chartered PLC's January 2025 7.625% CoCo, with preserved final documents,
independently checked formal consequences and actual execution in both targets?

The comparator is the current evidence-bound bank investigation, which leaves
other product distribution restrictions unresolved. The new conclusion is
**only the US purchaser route in the original offering**. It is neither SFC
product approval nor an executed transaction, an issuer allocation, or evidence
that either security is safe. The same hypothetical US-resident individual,
buying for their own account and not a qualified institutional buyer (QIB), is
used for both documents. USD 200,000 principal satisfies both published minimum
denominations. Each comparison uses its original offering terms; it is not a
claim that either primary offer remains open today.

## Assumptions and skeptical audit before implementation

- **Source baseline:** final priced issue documents, not a preliminary document,
  a base programme alone or a search snippet. Check the dated priced cover and
  operative selling restrictions. Standard Chartered's electronic cover notice
  contains completion boilerplate; inspect the dated offering inside it before
  classifying the edition. Its November 2025 web document has an unreadable
  protected wrapper; use the accessible January issue as a distinct security.
- **Investor premise:** a synthetic individual resident in the US, for their
  own benefit, not a QIB. US-person definitions are regime-specific. Neither
  wealth, Hong Kong PI/SPI status nor a US bank's involvement implies QIB status.
  Test the individual and beneficial account separately and preserve missing
  status evidence. This scenario is not an actual account description.
- **Approval boundary:** registered-offer access versus 144A/Regulation S
  purchaser eligibility. Do not treat absence of registration as an absolute
  lifetime ban; resale exceptions and later registration are outside this
  initial-offering decision. Do not treat SEC registration as SEC endorsement.
- **Fair comparison:** same purchaser and USD principal, two genuine contingent
  convertible issues, same gate and shared front end. The differing original
  offer dates are explicit; no assertion of simultaneous live availability.
- **No risk shortcut:** both conversion and statutory write-down/bail-in can
  cause severe or total loss. Permanent write-down versus conversion does not
  itself manufacture an SFC approval/denial pair.
- **Methods:** pinned existing RuleIR, Catala and Lean toolchains. Mathematical
  proof compares the shared expression to a separately written decision relation;
  native execution additionally checks the compiler paths. Neither proves
  English-source meaning. Synthetic counterfactuals must reverse the route
  decision when the relevant premises change, independently of issuer names.
- **Integration:** retain all seven bank layers and unresolved internal policy,
  sanctions identity, account facts and other product duties. A route pass must
  never grant transaction execution. An unsupported stage or route must abstain.

The naive plan to label a Swiss write-down CoCo rejected and a UK conversion CoCo
approved is rejected: the sources do not support that legal distinction. The
revised bounded offering-route comparison passes the skeptical audit. Its
earliest check is the pair of actual offering clauses, before writing code.
