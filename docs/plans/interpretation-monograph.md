# Interpretation assurance monograph: authoring and evidence plan

22 September 2026. User request: 5–10 chapters, 150–200 pages, self-contained
explanation and implementation-ready synthesis of ensemble interpretation,
discrepancy-triggered processing, bounded retries and complete residual-uncertainty
reporting. Canonical new document: `docs/monograph/monograph.tex` and its PDF.
Preserve the existing proposal, runtime, source snapshots, oracle and accepted runs.

## Reader and argument contract

Primary readers are private-bank compliance/legal specialists and engineers who
must jointly authorize and implement a regulatory control. Assume familiarity
with ordinary programming and bank operations, but no knowledge of formal legal
languages, argumentation semantics, MCTS, proof assistants or statistical
calibration. Explain these when needed and derive the manuscript's new formulas.
The running case is the unreviewed 23EC46 paragraph-10 experiment and its 22-case
oracle. Use synthetic contrasting offers, explicitly labelled; invent no legal
precedents, expert approvals or current-law determinations.

The argument progresses from a passing but non-independent oracle, through source
and interpretation uncertainty, to precise representations, competing hypotheses,
diverse evidence and a bounded resolution controller, then preservation to Java,
implementation, evaluation and operational change. The ensemble chapter is the
largest chapter. It must specify correlation risks, dissent preservation, issue
identity, budget accounting, termination, retry actions, repair validation,
invalidation, report contents and release conditions. An independent source
inventory and cross-reference closure address omissions even when all generators
agree. No finite ensemble establishes universal omission freedom.

## Skeptical audit before drafting

Wrong baseline: existing 154 tests and 22/729 second-circular results concern
engineering, not prose translation. Preserve that distinction throughout.
Proxy promotion: votes, proof success, beam score, roundtrip agreement and page
count cannot certify legal correctness, independence or readability.
Missing stopping rule: specify finite per-issue and global action budgets, elapsed
deadline, crash/restart accounting, cycles and no-progress handling; unresolved
material discrepancy blocks release. A compiler error rejects an encoding, not
necessarily the reading. Search exhaustion differs from resource exhaustion.
Unfair comparisons: evaluation must equalize sources, budget and reviewer access,
use held-out circular families and record coupled generators/reference authors.
Hidden assumptions: source authority and effective dates, operational mappings of
normative wording, unknown/conflict precedence, identity of incentive components,
human adjudication and provider independence all remain explicit.
Stale context: inspect current runtime closure and actual Java Map/String API;
older proposed record interfaces are not current implementation.
Environment: document builds use the available XeLaTeX/latexmk, CPU-only local
checks and retained PDFs. No GPU, paid model, bank write, Claude invocation or
application deployment is needed. No blanket generic MCTS reuse is claimed.
Meaningless successful artifact: a large PDF assembled from repetitive prose,
inflated layout or an unexplained specification appendix would fail this request.
Use ordinary A4 typography, substantive teaching and in-place examples; page
range is a delivery constraint, not a quality metric. Audit PASS after these
controls; proceed with a provisional complete manuscript under the user's request.

## Chapter plan and explanatory obligations

1. The bank's interpretation problem: distinguish source, meaning, implementation
   and action failures; explain why correct code can enforce the wrong rule.
2. The second circular from beginning to end: reconstruct scope, source inventory,
   fact definitions, all oracle families, mutations, missing dependencies and
   release refusal, with fresh disagreement scenarios.
3. What a proof can establish: propositions, typed expressions, unknown and
   conflict, conditional preservation, abstraction and evidence time.
4. Languages and translation methods: teach Catala, controlled English, legal
   metadata, contract state languages and neural/symbolic checks through the case.
5. Competing interpretations: legal theories, argumentation, breadth, search,
   discriminating questions, calibrated and structural uncertainty.
6. Proposed ensemble and bounded resolution: substantive synthesis and complete
   controller semantics with repair traces and common-mode failure analysis.
7. Verification to Java: independent oracles, witnesses, mutation, compiler
   obligations, actual interfaces, traces and transaction integration.
8. Implementable service design: schema relationships, lifecycle, durable actions,
   API/storage contracts, security boundaries and ordered implementation tasks.
9. Evaluation and confidence: adjudication, disagreement sets, controlled
   baselines, risk/coverage, sampling limits, cost and promotion criteria.
10. Deployment and continuing assurance: approved policy, source amendments,
    incidents, replay, responsibilities and a complete acceptance route.

Page estimates are provisional: target approximately 165–185 A4 pages including
contents and references, with 10 substantive chapters. Adjust substantive
coverage after the first complete build; do not change font or add blank pages
to manufacture the requested range. Keep code and lookup records subordinate to
the main teaching argument.

Initial typography choice after the first chapter pilot: standard 12-point A4
book text with 25 mm margins and 1.06 line spacing, to give the non-specialist
reviewer readable mathematical notation throughout a long document. The first
11-point compile was a layout probe, not an accepted format. Fix the 12-point
format for subsequent writing and meet the requested length through substantive
content, without later margin/font inflation or inserted blank pages.
