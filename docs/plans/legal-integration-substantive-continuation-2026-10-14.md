# Substantive continuation of the legal integration master

14 October 2026. This is a reviewed amendment to the **same** integration
master, not a replacement mission or a fresh allowance. Its question is whether
the selected tools can change a sound answer to the four original legal
questions, and which source or service limitations prevent that assessment.
The baseline is the retained 78-node master and its terminal state: 15
successful local nodes, seven blocked assessments, four failed acquisitions,
52 dependent blocks, 232 charged current calls, 41 separate historical calls,
and three consumed acquisition attempts per question. The four questions,
46 obligations, 35 readings, 12 cases and 24 selected-tool/question pairs stay
visible. The previous local check measured no request-construction overflow;
it did not qualify actual live semantics or a final answer.

## Evidence and authority

The primary promotion criterion remains a complete, source-reviewed original
answer and an actual matched product comparison for each applicable tool. A
wrong attribution, lost material exception, invalid source review, corrupted
artifact, stale code/source binding, exhausted attempt, or expired deadline
vetoes the affected promotion. Native execution, source-location matching,
test totals, and local token measurements are explanatory and can reject a bad
implementation; none alone establishes legal correctness or tool benefit.
No stochastic superiority ranking is planned. The result will retain the
current code commit/dirty identity, source revision, command, CPU environment,
resource ledger, elapsed time and artifacts. Missing client facts permit
conditional answers but prevent a factual compliance conclusion.

The local branch uses existing exact master commands with the context
interpreter and CPU hidden from GPU discovery. It adds **one attempt per new
local node**, not an extra attempt to any old node. The master deadline remains
19 October 2026 18:12:11.515205 UTC; the live deadline remains 18 October
2026 19:03:39.626294 UTC. The old 8,100-call ceiling and 232 charges are
unchanged. No live dispatch follows from this amendment. A later current
code/source/provider binding needs an explicit finite decision and must stop
before the earlier live deadline. A stale grant or a command that merely
removes an old guard is inadmissible.

## Skeptical pre-execution audit

**Revised PASS for this local amendment, conditional on implementation checks before
registration.** The first draft failed a source-baseline check: the added
October 2024 Handbook pages 16–18 contain Section I clause 6.8 on Product Key
Facts Statements, while footnote 1 points to clause 6.8 of the *Code on Unit
Trusts and Mutual Funds*. The retained Section II Code extract instead says
that a unit trust's trustee or appointed person must maintain a register of
holders. The plan now makes that source distinction an explicit check and
does not treat the generic Handbook 6.8 as an outsourcing warrant. The
previous plan's false-success risk was that a worker could
return an old blocker statement while the graph counted a completed package.
Each new worker must write inspectable source ownership, legal-account, graph
or binding evidence and reject empty/unsupported results. The comparator is
the archived current master, not a weakened fixture. The old acceptance
criterion remains complete original answers and matched effects; local checks
cannot promote themselves. The graph has no cycles, has completion-only paths
to review failures, and has distinct write resources. The earliest checks are
historical-byte/attempt/deadline validation, one wrong-locator negative case,
and native supported/adverse/unknown controls. A wrong rendered label or
ambiguous quote remains limited, not silently assigned. The source and service
environments are explicitly different: local text and tokenizer evidence do
not establish provider behavior. The risk that a current binding has no finite
authority is a campaign veto, not a reason to stop unrelated source work.

## Execution and stop

Register only after focused migration and worker tests pass. Run mission
`check`, `status`, `admit`, then master `run`; after every retained node the
master records mission history and refreshes. On interruption inspect the
persisted worker and result before redispatch. The program ends this local
pass as `BLOCKED` if no authorized node is ready and original answers or matched
effects remain absent. `COMPLETE` requires those original acceptance artifacts,
not just completion of the eleven new nodes. The final result must distinguish
source, legal, product, provider, authority and external-fact blockers and give
the exact next authorized step for each.
