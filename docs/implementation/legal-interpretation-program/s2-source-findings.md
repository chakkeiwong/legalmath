# S2 source findings — 6 October 2026

The official provisions support narrower obligations than the disputed paragraph
10 discharge claims. S2 retains their conditions in executable proposals and
keeps all original unresolved questions.

The UT Code introduction expressly says the Code does not have the force of law,
allows particular conditions and possible modifications, and supplies no actual
assessment dates. Section 6.8 allocates register maintenance to the scheme or,
for a unit trust, the trustee or its appointed person. Its address disclosure is
request-conditioned. Neither provision supplies general outsourcing permission,
a complete title rule or statutory liability.

UT 6.11C concerns a third party engaged in valuation. Its first sentence uses
“shall” for reasonable care, skill and diligence in selection, appointment and
ongoing monitoring. Its next sentence uses “should” for supervision and periodic
review. S2 separates these modalities; neither becomes a general tokenisation
supervision standard. This corrects a draft inventory that combined both under
SHOULD before execution.

The 23EC52 issuance rule preserves both issuance involvement and intention to
deal in or advise on the securities. Paragraphs 17–18 concern their named
intermediary activities and controls before those activities. Appendix factors
are explicitly non-exhaustive. Reconciliation, recovery and migration are
relevant evidence topics, not sufficient tests of proper records, title,
materiality or retrospective cure.

In 26EC22, paragraph 14 confirmation is represented separately from demonstration.
A, B and C remain competing demonstration proposals with different attachment,
object and standard assumptions. Paragraphs 15 and 16 have their own request
premises for audit/verification and legal opinion. No confidence threshold is
invented from footnote 5's “high level of confidence.”

## Judgments inspected

Full reasons, operative conclusions, schedules/annexure and footnotes of Re
Gatecoin Ltd [2023] HKCFI 914 and [2025] HKCFI 493 were read from the court-linked
mirror. Original court DOCX files were then retrieved from the exact judiciary
URLs supplied by the mirror. Exact passage checks use text extracted from those
original files. Raw responses and both extraction paths remain retained.

In the 2023 decision, paragraphs 59 and 82(3) recognize cryptocurrency as property
capable of forming trust property. Paragraphs 33–44, 65–71 and 82 distinguish that
capacity from whether Gatecoin actually held assets on trust under the applicable
terms. The non-consenting-customer exception matters. The liquidators' position
in paragraph 32 is not the court's final conclusion.

The 2025 decision expressly carries forward the earlier conclusions (paragraphs
1 and 16), but needed further customer and transaction evidence. Paragraphs 6–9
distinguish automated transactions from customer access and acceptance; an entry
after a terms-change date did not by itself settle consent. Paragraphs 43–48 and
95(6) address the particular lost-ETH trust claims and compensation. They do not
supply a general SFC incident/materiality or paragraph 10 standard. Paragraphs
63–94 and 95(7) distinguish Dan's obligations to Samara from whether Gatecoin
held the account or bitcoins on trust for Samara.

A textual tension remains in the 2025 source: paragraph 8(3) rejects the seventh
scenario as factually absent; paragraph 95(1) groups scenarios five to seven in
its exception wording; the annexure gives its own categories. S2 retains these
passages together and does not silently turn them into a single classifier.

These are Court of First Instance decisions in an exchange insolvency, with
specific customer terms and facts. Neither decides the 26EC22 disputes.
One subsequent decision has been inspected; subsequent-treatment completeness,
current statutory changes, governing-law transfer and scheme applicability are
not established. Propositions and transfer factors remain proposed source
readings, not proved ratio classifications.

## Search limits and rejected material

Initial official navigation guesses returned 404/500. The CFA's decided-case page
directed readers to HKLII; its page shell contained no judgment. The site's own
public application resource disclosed ordinary read-only search and judgment
endpoints, which were used within the original 24-request cap. No login or access
control was bypassed, and no model summarizer or submission endpoint was called.

The hypothesized Cheung citation [2009] HKCFA 63 proved to be Rank Profit
Industries Ltd v Secretary for Justice and was rejected. The retained search hits
and the 2023 CFA citation-locator response do not substitute for Cheung's full
reasons. That judgment is not admitted. Search failure does not establish absence
of relevant law. Indexes, search results, navigation code and error pages are
not treated as holdings.

The targeted responsibility/outsourcing query returned one lead, HKSAR v Leung
Wing Keung Albert and Another [2024] HKDC 768. Its full reasons were not inspected,
so it remains an unreviewed lead. The tokenisation response reported zero results
alongside “Exception occurred in handling search”; it is an application failure,
not evidence of no cases. Twenty-two HTTP requests were retained out of the
24-request ceiling. The original pre-amendment S2 plan is preserved under
`revisions/s2-plan-1.md` at its baseline hash.

The remaining product gaps are the actual assessment and actor facts,
scheme-specific conditions, sufficient discharge/propriety/title standards,
source-to-rule interpretation and broader or later authority. S3 must use these
specific gaps when integrating evidence and conditional standards into the actual
investigation route.
