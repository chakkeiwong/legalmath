# Gatecoin: what an ownership record establishes

The selected proposition is narrow: in deciding whether Gatecoin held the currencies on trust, ownership language in the exchange ledger did not override the operative terms and the objective relationship. This bears on the original requests for governing ownership law, record priority, actual ownership relationships and reconciliation evidence—particularly obligations 12, 14, 25 and 44. It does not determine paragraph 10 fulfillment.

## Source investigation

Full retained reasons, operative conclusions, schedule/annexure and notes were reread for **Re Gatecoin Ltd [2023] HKCFI 914**, Linda Chan J, 31 March 2023, and **Re Gatecoin Ltd [2025] HKCFI 493**, Linda Chan J, 24 January 2025. The text projections are `successor-s2/acquisition/gatecoin-2023/judgment-text.txt` and `gatecoin-2025/judgment-text.txt`. Selected propositions and the identified textual tension were cross-checked against `gatecoin-2023-official/text.txt` and `gatecoin-2025-official/text.txt`, extracted from retained official court DOCX responses. All paths are under `artifacts/legal-interpretation-program/`; source identities and exact passages are preserved with the execution result. No fresh retrieval or complete later-treatment search occurred.

The first decision was an application for directions under section 200(3) of the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance. The court determined the trust characterization and BlueFire issues; distribution questions were left for later. The relevant facts included different customer terms over time, pooled currencies, an internal exchange ledger, Gatecoin's ability to use currencies for its own business, and how customers accessed the platform. Those facts, rather than the mere use of blockchain, drove the relationship analysis.

The liquidators' submissions at 2023 paragraph 32 favored trust claims for Groups A and B. They are not the court's final result. Paragraphs 33–40 examined the 2018 terms, acceptance and the exception for customers who had not accessed or used the platform after the change. Paragraph 37 records the contrary arguments concerning notification, existing trusts, informed consent and the fair dealing rule. Paragraphs 38–40 explain why the court relied on acceptance while preserving the non-consenting-customer question.

Paragraphs 41–43 reason that, except for non-consenting customers, the 2018 terms did not create a trust. Relevant terms disclaimed fiduciary capacity, allowed Gatecoin to retain accretions and described pooled accounts; objective operational and accounting facts reinforced the conclusion. Pooling alone was not treated as a universal bar to trust: paragraphs 61–64 separately explain certainty of subject matter and objects in an identified fungible pool.

Paragraph 59 and the express conclusion at 82(3) recognize cryptocurrency as property capable of being trust property. That capacity is different from an actual trust relationship. Paragraph 65 quotes the principles that mutual intention depends on the objectively assessed agreement/relationship, and labels such as “trust”, “custody”, “ownership” and “title” are persuasive but not conclusive. Paragraphs 66–70 preserve the different earlier terms and the non-consenting customers. At 71(4), the ledger phrase tracking a user's ownership is expressly insufficient to displace the clear intention in other clauses. Paragraph 82(1), (2), (4) and (5) states the operative exceptions and conclusions. This supports the selected proposition in the facts of this case; it does not prove a universal hierarchy between every ledger and every contract.

The 2025 decision matters because applying the earlier conclusion required further facts. Paragraphs 6–9 distinguish entries made automatically, by Gatecoin or by third parties from customer access. Referral rewards, manual delisting updates, previously subscribed assets credited later, mining deposits and pre-change limit orders required distinct treatment. A post-change entry was not sufficient by itself to show post-change acceptance. The court's reasoning also included practical evidential considerations; it was not a universal classifier for digital events.

The same decision addresses other issues separately. Paragraphs 43–48 and 95(6), with annexure 1.8–1.9, characterize the particular lost-ETH claims as breach-of-trust compensation and set their quantum. They do not establish that every technical incident breaches paragraph 10 or that prompt remediation cures it. Paragraphs 49–94 inspect the Dan/Samara/Gatecoin relationships, account authority, correspondence, earlier adjudication and estoppel arguments. The trust obligation of Dan to Samara did not automatically create a Gatecoin-to-Samara trust. Paragraph 95(7) and annexure 1.10–1.11 reject that claim on the facts and procedural context. Paragraph 63's actual-authority reasoning and paragraph 86's finality reasoning must not be replaced by an abstract slogan that intermediary relationships are never transmissible.

There remains a source tension: 2025 paragraph 8(3) and 9(7) reject the seventh OTC scenario as factually absent; paragraph 95(1) groups scenarios five to seven under exception wording; annexure 1.1–1.4 supplies its own categories and does not add an OTC category. The official extraction retains this tension. This dossier makes no universal seven-scenario decision rule and does not silently harmonize it. The tension is outside the narrow 2023 selected proposition, but limits using the later case to build a complete classifier.

## Proposed transfer and strongest contrary

Supporting transfer argument: the target circular itself refers to records of ownership, and paragraph 17 calls for disclosure of legal/beneficial title and settlement finality. The original obligations ask whose rights are represented and which records establish them. Gatecoin supplies a concrete example where labels and entries were insufficient without terms, acceptance, actor relationships and dates. It therefore justifies preserving those dependencies and refusing a shortcut from “ledger says owner” to “this holder has this legal right”.

The strongest contrary to broader transfer is that Gatecoin concerned an exchange insolvency and particular currency holdings, while the target concerns Product Providers of SFC-authorised investment products in primary dealing. A token may represent rights in an underlying product rather than the cryptocurrency interest examined in Gatecoin. Different statutes, constitutive terms, registers, custody relationships and governing laws may control. The target might have an express statutory rule giving a register a specified effect. No inspected argument connects Gatecoin's trust test, compensation result or customer-acceptance directions to the fulfillment content of paragraph 10.

The missing transfer premises are consequently precise: identity of the right/asset; governing law and relevant statutory rules; the actual parties and capacities; operative product/contract terms and their effective acceptance; evidence linking the token/record to that right; and relevant subsequent treatment. The two CFI decisions and this one later application do not establish complete current-law coverage, a general hierarchy of precedent, or a proved ratio classification. Their source-linked characterizations remain reviewable legal analysis.

## Discriminating situations

| Situation | Supported use of the case | Unsupported shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Same ledger ownership label; different operative terms and acceptance | Inspect actual relationship before characterizing rights | Identical field label implies identical trust rights |
| Entry after changed terms, but a pre-change limit instruction or an automated credit | Ask whether the customer accessed/accepted; preserve contrary evidence | Entry timestamp alone establishes acceptance |
| Dan owes Samara fiduciary obligations while Gatecoin has a separate account relationship | Identify duty bearer, beneficiary, property, terms and procedural findings separately | A duty between two parties automatically proves a duty against a third |
| Product record mismatch despite supervised outsourced registrar | Specify whose rights, authoritative records and relevant standard are affected | Gatecoin supplies an SFC materiality threshold or paragraph 10 discharge test |

Conclusion: accept the case as support for a precise evidence dependency and as a counterexample to the proposed ledger-label shortcut. Defer any target-specific ownership conclusion until its legal and factual premises are supplied. Reject transfer of Gatecoin into a general paragraph 10 fulfillment or incident-liability rule. The existing recorded-event adapter already preserves the relevant absence of sufficiency and relationship evidence; a concrete failure is required before changing it.
