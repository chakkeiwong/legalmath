# Paragraph 14 and footnote 5: request, object and standard

The principal dispute is attachment. Confirmation, requested demonstration, requested third-party audit or verification, and requested legal opinion are separate acts. The source does not permit treating evidence of one as fulfillment of all the others.

## Source argument

The complete retained main text is unit `d3b41d4d7e61c.u50.4316` in S4 `development-inputs.json`: “Product Providers should confirm and, upon SFC’s request, demonstrate to the SFC’s satisfaction the management and operational soundness of the tokenisation arrangement, record keeping of ownership and the integrity of the smart contracts5.”

Footnote 5 (`u89.8054`) says providers “should confirm and demonstrate to the SFC that, at a minimum, they comply with the requirements as set out in paragraphs 10 to 13 above and the smart contracts are not subject to any contract vulnerabilities or security flaws with a high level of confidence.” It does not repeat the request condition. The question is how that footnote relates to the main-text trigger, subjects and evidential standard.

| Proposal | Trigger for demonstration | Object | Standard and unresolved point |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Applicable paragraph 14 request | Main-text management, operational soundness, ownership records and smart-contract integrity | SFC satisfaction; precise effect of footnote 5 on object/standard is unresolved |
| B | Standing duty, under the proposal that the footnote's unqualified wording independently governs | At least paragraphs 10–13 compliance and absence of vulnerabilities/security flaws | High confidence retained as qualitative source language; this reading must answer the contrary express request wording |
| C | Applicable paragraph 14 request | Footnote minimum content read with main-text subjects | Main-text SFC satisfaction plus footnote minimum/high-confidence language; the attachment remains an interpretation premise |

A has direct support in the main sentence's placement of “upon SFC’s request” immediately before “demonstrate”. Its strongest difficulty is that simply ignoring the footnote would lose its minimum-content language. The retained A rule therefore leaves that relationship unresolved; it is not an assertion that the footnote has no effect.

B has support in the footnote's unqualified “confirm and demonstrate” and “at a minimum”. Its strongest contrary is the main sentence's explicit request condition. The inspected text supplies no priority rule proving that the footnote removes that condition. It remains a rival, not a default selected because it is more demanding.

C attempts to read both passages together: the main text governs when demonstration is demanded and the footnote specifies minimum content. This preserves both pieces of text, but that coherence is an interpretive argument rather than a proof. The strongest contrary is the absence of request language in the footnote. C also requires care not to flatten the meaning of “high level of confidence”: whether it qualifies the demonstration, absence-of-flaws proposition, or a wider evidential assessment needs an explicit source argument. No confidence percentage follows from the retained words.

Under the current confirmation proposal, the inserted request phrase modifies demonstration rather than confirmation. This is separately encoded as `p14-confirmation`, with its own interpretation premise. It says a confirmation duty applies; it does not prove that a confirmation was delivered or that its content is true. Footnote 5's effect on confirmation content remains part of the attachment dispute.

Paragraph 15 (`u53.4565`) has its own request condition for third-party audit **or** verification of the specified subjects. Paragraph 16 (`u56.4803`) has a separate request for a satisfactory legal opinion supporting the application. A single actual letter could contain several requests, but evidence must establish each object. One request is not inferred from another, and obtaining an audit does not itself establish SFC satisfaction or legal opinion adequacy.

## Distinguishing inputs and conditional consequences

Hold actor, applicability, period and each proposed interpretation fixed as explicit positive premises. Vary request separately:

| Paragraph 14 request | A | B | C | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supported true | Duty conditionally derives | Duty conditionally derives | Duty conditionally derives | Duty conditionally derives |
| Supported false | Does not derive | Duty conditionally derives | Does not derive | Duty conditionally derives |
| Missing | Does not derive; request unknown | Duty conditionally derives | Does not derive; request unknown | Duty conditionally derives |
| Conflicting | Does not derive; conflict retained | Duty conditionally derives | Does not derive; conflict retained | Duty conditionally derives |

Non-derivation is not a conclusion that no duty exists. B's result rests on its distinct standing-duty assumption, not on using missing evidence as proof that a request was absent. Even when all three duties derive, A and C remain different in object/standard. Matching trigger truth is not equivalence of their legal meanings.

To test object independently, suppose a submission discusses operational soundness and smart-contract integrity but gives no supported assessment of paragraph 12 risk controls. The authored A duty still names its main-text subjects; C's minimum-content proposal expressly includes paragraph 12. That difference identifies an evidence dependency, but no supplied rule proves that this submission satisfies A either. To test standard independently, suppose the same report states high confidence while SFC satisfaction has not been evidenced. Neither statement supplies the other. To test distinct acts, suppose the provider confirms, has a paragraph 15 audit request, and lacks a paragraph 14 demonstration request or paragraph 16 opinion request. The current rules keep those statuses separate.

Required inputs are each request's exact content, issuing authority, recipient, activity and interval; the actual confirmation and demonstration; evidence for the underlying paragraph 10–13 propositions; technical examination of the relevant smart-contract version; and the correspondence evidencing any SFC satisfaction. Audit/verification and legal opinion have their own documents, scope and sufficiency judgments. Absence of a known flaw, no request, or a report's confidence phrase cannot fill these missing inputs.

Conclusion: A/B/C remain competing source interpretations. Existing rules can compute their different conditional triggers while preserving their consequence text and standards. The focused diagnostic varies request and interpretation states and checks that unrelated request kinds do not substitute. No new attachment engine or numeric confidence threshold is justified by this dossier.
