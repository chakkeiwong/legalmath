# Paragraph 10: retained responsibility, conduct and outcomes

The retained circular supports a continuing responsibility expectation despite outsourcing. It does not, in the inspected text, supply a complete rule deciding when supervision, an operational defect and remediation together amount to fulfillment. The productive result is therefore a conditional comparison of distinct proposed standards, with the missing judgments identified.

## Source and question

Source: 26EC22, revised 20 April 2026, retained in S4 `development-inputs.json`, unit `d3b41d4d7e61c.u38.3432` (paragraph 10), read with `u85.7210` (footnote 1). The original is `docs/papers/Circular on tokenisation of SFC-authorised investment products_SFC(2026).json`. Source hashes and exact texts are retained in the execution evidence. The selected question concerns one identified Product Provider, product, adopted primary-dealing arrangement and interval, not overall circular compliance.

Paragraph 10 says Product Providers “should remain and be ultimately responsible” for management, operational soundness and ownership record keeping, “regardless of any outsourcing arrangement”. Footnote 1 says “fully responsible, despite any outsourcing arrangement”, refers illustratively to UT Code 6.8, and refers to Appendix Part A factors “where applicable”. These words support continuing responsibility and deny outsourcing an automatic exculpatory effect. They do not establish that every delegation is permitted or that the Product Provider must perform each task personally.

Paragraph 11 (`u41.3664`) separately requires proper ownership-interest records and operational compatibility. Paragraph 12 (`u44.3887`) addresses appropriate risk controls and continuity. Paragraph 14 (`u50.4316`) concerns confirmation/demonstration, while paragraph 19 (`u71.6015`) concerns at least one competent member of staff with relevant experience and expertise to operate and/or supervise and manage the new risks. The separate formulations are material to the competing arguments.

## Rival arguments

The allocation proposal emphasizes the grammatical focus on who remains responsible when another entity performs a function. Separate performance requirements in paragraphs 11–13 support treating retained accountability as a distinct control. On this proposal a provider can retain effective ultimate responsibility even if a registrar's defect breaches a separate records requirement. The strongest contrary argument is that responsibility *for soundness and records*, reinforced by “fully” and footnote 1's investor-ownership rationale, may demand substantive discharge, making retained powers alone insufficient.

The conduct proposal gives responsibility substantive content through actual management, supervision and corrective action. Paragraphs 12 and 19 make controls, expertise and supervision relevant, and the phrase “operate and/or supervise” helps distinguish oversight from personally executing every task. It could allow an incident consistent with adequately assessed preventive and remedial conduct. Its strongest weakness is that these neighboring requirements do not by themselves prove that adequate conduct exhausts paragraph 10. “Effective stewardship” cannot become an answer merely by naming it as a new fact.

The outcome proposal treats responsibility for operational soundness and proper records as requiring achieved adequacy as well as retained accountability. Paragraphs 11 and 14 and footnote 1's stated rationale support examining this reading. An established failure of the selected outcome standard would defeat its conjunction even with diligent supervision. Its strongest weakness is the possible importation of neighboring duties into paragraph 10. Nothing inspected establishes that every incident means inadequacy, or supplies an absolute guarantee or a civil strict-liability rule.

No inspected argument proves the unique governing reading. The 35 retained statements remain individual proposals: some add actor/component coverage, separate responsibility subjects, temporal continuity or exception attachment. Their shared vocabulary does not erase those differences.

## What must be supplied before the conditions can be evaluated

For a declared comparison, let R mean *assessed effective retention* across each relevant subject and interval, C mean *assessed conduct satisfying an explicitly proposed standard*, and O mean *assessed adequacy of the required outcomes*. These are judgments, not raw observations. Three illustrative conditions are R, R∧C and R∧O; the retained outcome formula further separates sound management/operation from proper records. These expressions describe the proposed questions only after their definitions, scope and assessment period have been aligned. They are not new legal rules admitted by this dossier.

| Concept | Observable or documentable inputs | Judgment that remains |
|---|---|---|
| Retained powers | Appointment/delegation terms; information and audit rights; access to records; escalation, intervention and replacement powers; dated allocation for each function | Whether those powers amount to effective ultimate responsibility and whether they were operative throughout |
| Exercised supervision | Reports obtained and reviewed; alerts; escalation decisions; actual interventions; dates; unresolved discrepancies; staff coverage | Whether the actions, frequency and response were adequate for the risks and role |
| Competence | Named staff, relevant experience, duties, training, demonstrated tasks, access and availability | Whether that evidence establishes relevant expertise and competence for this arrangement; no numerical score is supplied |
| Material records failure | What entries disagree; affected holders/rights/amounts; duration; settlement effects; evidence of loss or inability to establish rights | Whether the discrepancy is material under the applicable standard; no arbitrary loss or duration threshold is introduced |
| Proper records and sound operation | Reconciliation results, system behavior, error scope, governing title/finality terms, recovery tests and coverage | Whether those observations satisfy the independently identified legal/professional standard |
| Remediation | Corrected entries, recovered access, completed actions, residual issues and dates | Whether recovery cures only future performance or also affects an earlier deficiency |

The first unsupported step is the source-to-standard relation. The next is the relation between the supplied observations and the evaluative judgments. A proof of the final conjunction establishes neither. UT 6.11C's valuation-specific care and monitoring language cannot silently supply a universal tokenisation cadence. Non-exhaustive Appendix factors cannot supply an exhaustive discharge checklist.

## Distinguishing scenarios

| Scenario and explicit assumptions | Allocation R | Conduct R∧C | Outcome R∧O | What the example establishes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Registrar defect; *assume* effective retention and adequate supervision/remediation, but an established failure of the selected records outcome over the assessment interval | True conditionally | True conditionally | False conditionally | Different proposed standards can answer differently on aligned supplied judgments; it does not establish which standard governs |
| Nominal contract, no effective powers or supervision; *assume* this fails the supplied retention and conduct criteria despite a sound snapshot | False conditionally | False conditionally | False conditionally | A contractual label and good outcome cannot substitute for effective retention |
| Effective retained powers, no exercised supervision, sound outcomes; *assume* R true, C false, O true under distinct standards | True conditionally | False conditionally | True conditionally | Retained capacity and actual exercise are not interchangeable; some readings may themselves include exercise within R, so this assignment is not licensed for all 35 readings |
| Only “nominal responsibility, no supervision” observed, with no retention criterion | Unresolved | Unresolved | Unresolved | Do not turn the observation into R=false without a criterion; possible outcome success does not resolve it |
| Assessment interval unknown | No supported period-wide answer | No supported period-wide answer | No supported period-wide answer | A date on the source or event is not the requested assessment period |

The runtime diagnostic evaluates the existing formulas on declared inputs and checks that the actual scoped evaluator preserves interpretation and interval qualifications. The three retained formulas already distinguish allocation from outcomes and partial responsibility allocation. Adding another opaque Boolean would not explain how to assess R, C or O.

Conclusion: the conditional distinctions are expressible with the current machinery. Paragraph 10's standard, its relationship to adjacent requirements, and the assessment of conduct/outcomes remain unresolved legal premises. The required factual inputs are specified above. This supports a bounded conditional analysis, not a finding of fulfillment for an actual provider.
