# Follow-up source review — 4 October 2026

Seventeen additional public requests were recorded, taking the cumulative count
from 170 to 187 of the authorised 212. Twenty-five remain. The queue and policy
snapshots are preserved separately from the earlier studies.

Five Google responses were navigation shells. Five Bing RSS responses contained
results unrelated to their queries; these provide no evidence about the searched
legal questions. The earlier Dana issuer route returned no readable body. Further
broad queries and repeated blocked FMA/Banco de Portugal endpoints were stopped.

The official Normattiva pages identify Law 130 of 30 April 1999 and Royal Decree
267 of 16 March 1942 and display the requested historical dates. The retained Law
130 Article 4 page is explicitly the version in force from 29 May 1999 to
21 February 2014, requested as at 1 June 2009. Article 4(3) expressly excludes
Article 67 for payments by assigned debtors to the assignee. This supports the
narrow distinction already disclosed in the 2009 RMBS prospectus.

The explanatory notes under Article 4 reproduce an older Article 67. They are
not a substitute for the actual 2009 operative Article 67. The first two bankruptcy
article URNs returned the decree's introductory article, not Articles 65 and 67
in its annex. Exact article links extracted from the official historical page
returned HTTP 500. All four responses remain labelled as unsuitable for those
specific operative articles. The prospectus's Article 65 discussion therefore
continues to supply the reviewed scenario; the full historical-law check remains
open.

No new PDF offering or regulatory decree was recovered. The Portuguese decision
and Annex 2B, original HETA decree and debt list, Dana 2013 offer, Lloyds 2009 offer
and trust deed, Ukraine 2013 offer, Popular/Snoras originals and the exact Italian
bankruptcy articles remain open. These are source gaps, not evidence that the
candidate's local engineering repairs failed. Acquisition resumes only when a
specific new route can resolve one of these questions.
