It closes no obligation. Exact passages, Unicode codepoint intervals, source

The additional source distinction concerns **what supervision must establish
when an applicable intermediary requirement is invoked**. The existing dossier
already rejects the inference from a supervision event to adequate supervision.
Paragraphs 17–18 and 23–24 of 23EC52 give that objection a more specific basis:
understanding and assessing controls before engaging in an activity is a
different proposition from recording an oversight event; effective mitigation
is a different proposition again. Their relevance depends on the provider's
role and activity. They do not decide the separate dispute about fulfillment of
paragraph 10 of 26EC22.

## Source and applicability

23EC52 paragraph 17 concerns intermediaries that deal in, advise on, or manage
portfolios investing in Tokenised Securities. It calls for due diligence on
issuers, their third-party vendors/service providers, and the arrangement's
features and risks. Paragraph 18 says intermediaries should “understand and be
satisfied with the controls implemented” to manage ownership and technology
risks **before** engaging in the related activities. Paragraph 12 separately
calls for due skill, care and diligence. Paragraph 13 preserves responsibility
for the overall operation despite outsourcing where the intermediary issues,
or is substantially involved in issuing, securities it intends to deal in or
advise on. These are related requirements with different predicates.

In the section headed “Complex product categorisation”, paragraph 23 says:

> Further, intermediaries are required to ensure that the new risks arising from
> the use of the new technology should be effectively mitigated and would not
> impact on investors. Hence, tokenisation should not alter the complexity of
> the underlying security.

Paragraph 24 then begins:

> Based on the above (ie, the risks arising from the use of the new technology
> are effectively mitigated), whether a Tokenised Security is a complex product
> or not is based on an assessment of the complexity of its underlying
> traditional security.

The remainder directs assessment by reference to the named distribution and
conduct guidance, and addresses the requirements for distributing a complex
product. The passages therefore connect the stated mitigation premise to the
see-through complexity approach. They do not supply an exhaustive method for
testing controls, an incident tolerance, or a rule making every incident change
the product's classification.

There is a source-based route to considering these provisions in the provider
analysis. Paragraph 9 of 26EC22 says the “relevant requirements” of the Tokenised
Securities Circular should also be satisfied. Paragraph 18 expressly includes
Product Providers distributing their own products among distributors subject
to intermediary requirements. Conversely, 23EC52 paragraphs 4 and 16 direct
readers to the investment-products circular. These links support reading the
requirements together **where the actor, security and activity fall within
their scope**. “Relevant” does not make every intermediary provision applicable
to every Product Provider. Issuance, distribution, advice and portfolio
management must be identified separately; the applicable edition and assessment
period remain unresolved in the retained qualification.

## Consequence for the answer

For a provider distributing its own tokenised product, a justified answer may
need to address both continuing provider responsibility and the applicable
distribution requirements. It should state what evidence supports the
pre-activity understanding and assessment of the issuer/vendor controls, then
what supports the mitigation premise when relying on the complexity approach.
A dated meeting minute proves neither by itself.

Consider a declared hypothetical: staff attended a meeting and signed a
supervision log, but nobody assessed whether compromised keys could prevent
holders from establishing their ownership interests. The log alone does not
establish the understanding and satisfaction described in paragraph 18. This
does not prove that the controls were ineffective; that is a separate question
requiring evidence about the particular arrangement and risk.

Now assume staff did assess the controls with appropriate care, yet evidence
establishes that an identified ownership risk remains unmitigated. The conduct
evidence and effectiveness evidence point to different propositions. A product
answer cannot use the former alone to assert the mitigation premise in
paragraphs 23–24. Whether the observed deficiency establishes nonfulfillment of
26EC22 paragraph 10, breaches another provision, affects a particular
classification, or is cured by subsequent remediation requires the relevant
legal standard and timing. None of those conclusions is supplied by this
example.

This refines the reconciliation argument. Matching records can share an error
about rights; documented supervision can coexist with an unassessed or
ineffective control. In both cases the observation must be related to the
substantive proposition the answer claims to establish. The sources now give
more specific actor, activity and timing content for the supervision branch.

## Strongest contrary and remaining question

The strongest contrary reading is that paragraphs 23–24 explain the regulatory
basis for the complexity approach; they are not an independently specified
provider-discharge test. Paragraph 18 uses “understand and be satisfied”, while
paragraph 12 specifies a care standard. Reading those provisions as an absolute
guarantee would erase their context and import a consequence the inspected text
does not state. Even if an applicable intermediary requirement is unsatisfied,
that does not itself select allocation, conduct or outcome as the unique meaning
of paragraph 10.

Before this supplement, the source argument distinguished observations from
assessed retention/conduct/outcomes and rejected reconciliation as a sufficient
test. After it, the answer can identify a specific **pre-activity control
assessment** and the **effective-mitigation premise** of the complexity
discussion, conditional on intermediary applicability. This is a source
refinement, not an observed improvement to the product's answer. It supplies no
numerical threshold or universal checklist and leaves the original rival
readings intact.

The decisive missing authority is still one addressing whether a specified
records or operational deficiency, despite competent retained supervision and
remediation, means paragraph 10 itself was unfulfilled during the relevant
period. Further source research should seek that proposition directly. Client
facts are needed to apply such a standard, but their absence does not prevent
this conditional source analysis.
