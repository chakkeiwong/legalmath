# Proposed supplement to the fixed P14 answer

Status: source-reviewed draft for the next safe acceptance checkpoint. The fixed
answer and its hash are unchanged. This supplement has not been consumed by the
running product or passed its fresh general and dependency reviews.

The existence of a smart-contract audit does not establish the assurance sought
by paragraph 14 or footnote 5. Paragraph 14 concerns the integrity of the smart
contracts in the tokenisation arrangement, while footnote 5 connects the showing
to compliance with paragraphs 10–13 and to the absence of contract vulnerabilities
or security flaws with a high level of confidence. The narrower and coordinated
broader readings of that confidence qualification remain as explained in the
fixed answer; neither supplies a numerical threshold or proves SFC satisfaction.

The intermediary appendix gives more specific guidance on audit reliance. It
addresses the smart contract deployed in the securities' operation and requires
an intermediary relying on a third-party smart-contract audit to be able to
demonstrate that reliance is reasonable. The product circular's reference to the
appendix preserves its where-applicable qualification and the actors' different
roles. The appendix should therefore not be restated as an unconditional audit
procurement duty on every Product Provider, particularly where paragraphs 15
and 16 state separate request conditions.

For an audit to support a claim about the contract actually deployed, there must
be evidence connecting what was audited to what was deployed and operated. That
connection is an evidential inference from the object of the assurance, rather
than a quoted standalone statutory version-control rule. Relevant evidence may
include the audited code or build identity, deployed contract and upgrade state,
audit scope and assumptions, exclusions, unresolved findings and remediation,
and changes since the audit. Those examples are means of testing correspondence;
they are not an invented exhaustive regulatory checklist.

If the evidence establishes that the audit covers the relevant deployed contract
and configuration, that relevant findings and later changes have been addressed,
and that reliance on the auditor is justified in the applicable arrangement, the
audit can contribute to the relevant showing. It still does not, by itself,
establish all operational responsibilities, an SFC request, the sufficiency of
the entire demonstration, or SFC satisfaction. If version correspondence,
coverage or justified reliance is unknown, that part of the assurance remains
unestablished. Conditional source analysis can proceed while identifying precisely
which evidence is needed. This supplements the fixed answer's distinction between
procurement and substantive fulfillment without changing its request-trigger rivals.
