# Gatecoin: an entry may be evidence without being conclusive

9 October 2026. Original question `gatecoin`; original obligations 12, 14, 25 and
44. Exact identities remain in the mission register. This is a focused extension
of the completed full reading of the 2023 and 2025 decisions. Source excerpts,
official-text hashes and numbered mirror passages are retained in
`artifacts/legal-mission-control/2026-10-09/continuation/gatecoin-transfer/source-excerpts.json`.

The earlier dossier correctly rejected an automatic inference from a post-change
ledger entry to customer acceptance of changed terms. The further point is that
**the 2025 court did not give all such entries zero evidential weight**. It
distinguished categories with different prima facie treatment, rebuttal conditions
and evidence directions. A product that merely changes “entry implies acceptance”
to “entry is irrelevant” would still misstate the decision.

## What the court actually distinguished

The 2018 terms became effective on 6 March 2018, the Relevant Date in the decision.
The classification concerns non-consenting customers (NCCs) in the Gatecoin
liquidation and the application of its terms; it is not a general statutory test
of consent by every token holder.

| Retained scenario | Treatment in the reasons | Evidence or qualification that changes the analysis |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4: referral rewards, delisting updates, earlier subscriptions credited later, mining deposits | Paragraph 8(1) treats these customers as NCCs unless there is evidence that they accessed/used the platform to effect the transactions. | Category membership and the nature of the event matter. Paragraph 9(2), for example, distinguishes a manual delisting update from the customer's later login to withdraw TRX. |
| 5–6: GEN private sale and purported limit orders | Paragraph 8(2) describes transactions that on their face required platform use and prima facie fall outside NCC status, with an exception for proof of an earlier or non-platform instruction. | Paragraph 9 supplies particular facts. The GEN sale began in May 2018; a prior subscription cannot simply be invented. For limit orders, paragraph 9(6) requires proof that the order was placed before the Relevant Date. |
| 7: claimed OTC transactions | Paragraphs 8(3) and 9(7) say there was no factual basis for such transactions and reject NCC treatment on that basis. | Paragraph 95(1) nevertheless groups scenarios 5–7 within exception language. That inconsistency remains unresolved. |

The categories are substantive. Paragraph 9(1) also explains the practical cost
and difficulty of investigating when referral links were clicked and rewards
processed. Treating those customers as NCCs rests partly on that evidential
setting, not on a universal proposition that referral credits can never follow
customer action.

The Annexure must be retained with the reasons. Paragraph 1.1 identifies the first
four categories as not requiring post-date platform access. Paragraph 1.2(a)
identifies the GEN sale, starting in May, as requiring it. Paragraph 1.2(b) treats
the claimed limit order similarly unless the customer proves an earlier order.
Paragraphs 1.3–1.4 preserve the exception and require notification and supporting
evidence for the limit-order claim within 21 days. That deadline is a direction
in this case, not a general limitation period or tokenisation rule. The Annexure
does not supply an OTC category that resolves the scenario-seven wording.

It would therefore be wrong to flatten paragraph 8(2) into a universal exception
equally available on any invented facts in scenarios 5–7. The general description,
the specific factual reasons and the operative directions need to remain linked.
This task supplies no rule automatically choosing one conflicting passage as
legally controlling in scenario seven.

## Conditional tests for the product

Consider two entries with the same posting date. One is an automatic mining
credit in the court's scenario 4; the other is the execution of an alleged limit
order in scenario 6. If the evidence establishes the first category and no
contrary access evidence, paragraph 8(1)'s direction supports NCC treatment. In
the second category, absence of proof of the earlier order does not supply the
exception described in paragraph 9(6) and the Annexure. The same timestamp is
compatible with different classifications because the action, evidence and
case-specific direction differ.

Two qualifications are essential. First, unknown category membership cannot be
treated as established scenario-4 membership or as a negative finding about
customer action. Second, applying the court's direction about an evidential
default is different from asserting that a missing fact is false. The product
must identify the direction and its scope if it uses that default. This is
precisely the kind of premise a generic argument solver will not discover from
an untyped timestamp.

A second test concerns relevant contrary evidence: establish a delisting entry
and then add evidence of the customer's login to withdraw TRX. A system that
retains “automatic entry, therefore NCC” after receiving that evidence loses the
express paragraph-9 qualification. Conversely, it should not infer such a login
merely because the entry appears after the terms changed.

The 2023 ledger-label conclusion and the 2025 distinct-party reasoning remain
unchanged. Property capable of being held on trust is not an actual trust; an
obligation of Dan to Samara does not by itself establish a Gatecoin-to-Samara
trust. Paragraph 86 also records the binding earlier determination and absence
of an appeal in that proceeding at that time. It is not evidence that no appeal
or later treatment exists as of October 2026.

## Transfer and remaining work

The supported product-development use is to preserve event category, actor,
instruction date, platform action, contrary evidence, and the provenance of any
prima facie direction. This is a test of faithful reasoning about this retained
decision. It is not a transplant of the NCC directions or a 21-day requirement
into paragraph 10, a Product Provider's title regime, or another contractual
acceptance dispute.

The proposed target transfer still needs the asset/right, governing law,
constitutive terms, relevant parties, actual record role and applicability of
the cited reasoning. A current-authority claim additionally needs a dated search
for subsequent treatment. The specific unresolved-source request is for an
official correction, further direction, appeal or reasoned later treatment that
addresses the inconsistency between paragraphs 8/9 and 95 concerning scenario
seven. Merely finding another summary of Gatecoin would not resolve it.

Before: the dossier rejected the timestamp shortcut and identified the source
tension. After: the source analysis supplies the different defeasible directions
and their evidence conditions for the recorded scenarios, and checks them against
the Annexure. The product can now be tested for the opposite error of discarding
the evidential effect of an entry after rejecting its conclusiveness. This is a
limited additional source argument; no original obligation is closed.

Red-team assessment: a neat decision table can overstate what the judgment says.
The distinctions above remain linked to the court's precise factual/procedural
setting and operative directions. A contrary direction or correction can change
the classification. The weakest evidence is generalization to the target
arrangement; the strongest is the express difference between the scenario groups.
