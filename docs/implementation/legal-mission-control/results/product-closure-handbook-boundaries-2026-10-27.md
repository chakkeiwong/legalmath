## Custody, delegation and the actual actor

UT 4.1 requires an acceptable trustee for a unit trust or custodian for a mutual
fund corporation on an ongoing basis. UT 4.3 concerns independent audit of
compliance with applicable financial-resource requirements. It is not a
smart-contract audit rule. The capital exception in 4.4 continues onto PDF page
31 and must not be inferred from the incomplete end of page 30.

UT 4.5(a)(i) allocates custody or control of scheme property to the trustee or
custodian. The trust is for holders in a unit trust and for the scheme in a mutual
fund corporation, in accordance with the constitutive documents. Its note
requires a proper book record in the scheme's name for property that by nature
cannot be held in custody. Paragraph (ii) addresses registration of registrable
assets in the name of, or to the order of, the trustee or custodian.

Paragraph (iii) makes the trustee/custodian liable for acts and omissions of
nominees, agents and delegates relating to assets forming part of scheme
property. The note requires prudential regulation and supervision of those
appointed for custody or safekeeping unless otherwise accepted by the Commission.
It also requires the Commission to be satisfied with the overall arrangement's
proper and adequate safeguards, taking relevant requirements into account.
Application therefore needs the actor's legal capacity, the delegation
relationship, the act or omission, and its connection with scheme assets.
A vendor relationship or the label Product Provider alone does not prove those
facts. Nor does a general record-keeping duty establish this particular liability.

Paragraph (iv) separates three segregation categories: management company,
investment delegates and their connected persons; the trustee/custodian and
custody-chain nominees, agents and delegates; and their other clients. The
continuation on page 32 permits an omnibus account only within the third category
and only with adequate safeguards in line with international standards and best
practices, proper recording and frequent appropriate reconciliations. It does
not excuse segregation from the custodian's own property. Paragraph (v)
separately requires appropriate ownership-verification measures. Reconciliation
alone is not proof of ownership verification. UT 4.5(c) and UT 6.11A–6.12 have
already been separately reviewed for valuation; this supplement preserves that
actor-specific distinction.

## Retirement and independence

UT 4.6 permits retirement only with a new trustee/custodian appointment and prior
Commission approval, expressly referring to 11.1. Retirement and the incoming
office must take effect together. The missing cross-reference is now inspected:
UT 11.1(b), PDF page 94, requires proposed changes of trustee/custodian, management
company, investment delegates, Hong Kong Representative and their regulatory
status to be submitted for prior approval. UT 11.1A provides for the Commission
to determine notification and notice period for changes under 11.1; revised
Hong Kong offering documents require prior authorization.

UT 11.2, page 95, calls for notice in the offer languages and a reasonable period.
One month is the normal expectation, subject to the stated exceptions, a longer
applicable requirement and Commission agreement; it is not an unconditional
one-month rule. Shorter notice may be permitted for demonstrably beneficial
changes; clarification or administrative changes normally require notice as
soon as reasonably practicable unless otherwise specified. The rule also
addresses additional notice for overseas-domiciled schemes. UT 11.2A retains
11.4/11.5 qualifications and the Commission's ability to require draft review;
notices concerning 11.1 matters must be approved before distribution. This
review does not assert that every notice can bypass approval or that 11.4/11.5
have been fully researched.

UT 4.7 requires independence of trustee/custodian and management company. The
4.8 route concerns corporate bodies sharing an ultimate holding company. Shared
ownership alone is insufficient: neither is the other's subsidiary, they have
no common director, and both sign the independent-dealing undertaking. The note
adds functional independence of custody/safekeeping and management, with systems
and controls; its examples include independent board, governance/reporting and
operational teams. Page 34 marks 4.8(b) deleted. There is no alternative current
route to reconstruct from the deleted marker. Application needs the corporate
relationships, directors, undertakings and actual arrangements. Those facts
remain open; their absence does not prevent stating the conditional rule.

## Management-company risk systems

UT 5.10 names the management company. Paragraph (f) concerns proper systems to
monitor and measure the risks of scheme positions and their contribution to
overall portfolio risk. Note (1), page 38, requires suitable and adequate
systems for all relevant risks, including financial derivatives, commensurate
with the nature and scale of transactions and investment activities, taking the
retail nature and risk profile into account. They must handle normal and
exceptional circumstances, including extreme markets, and remain effective at
all times.

The note continues on page 39: suitable and adequate resources, including human
resources; effective liquidity policies with stress testing where applicable
and consideration of strategy, investors, liquidity, obligations and redemption;
internal credit-risk assessment in which external ratings are one factor and
not mechanically relied upon; and applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
Omitting this continuation understates the parent requirement. These management-
company duties cannot automatically be relabelled as trustee, intermediary or
vendor duties. Actual adequacy needs evidence of the relevant actor, systems,
resources and portfolio, not merely a statement that supervision occurred.
