## P10: responsibility and fulfillment

Paragraph 10 of 26EC22 makes the Product Provider ultimately responsible for
management, operational soundness and ownership record keeping despite
outsourcing; footnote 1 reinforces full responsibility and explains the
ownership-record concern. That allocation does not imply personal performance
of every function. Paragraph 11 separately calls for proper ownership-interest
records and operational compatibility, while paragraph 19 concerns competent
staff able to operate and/or supervise. These different formulations matter
when asking whether **retained responsibility itself was fulfilled**.

Three live rivals remain. An allocation reading asks whether ultimate
accountability remained effective; a conduct reading also tests actual
management, supervision and correction; an outcome reading asks whether the
arrangement and records met an applicable substantive standard. A registrar
defect with competent oversight can produce different conditional answers
under those readings. The strongest contrary to allocation alone is the text's
responsibility *for* soundness and records, footnote 1's ownership rationale,
and paragraph 11's separate substantive requirement. The strongest contrary
to making every defect conclusive is that the inspected text supplies neither
a universal incident threshold nor a rule importing each adjacent duty into
paragraph 10. None of the retained sources selects a unique discharge test.

For an actual provider, the product must identify the applicable edition and
period; the relevant provider, arrangements and outsourced functions; powers
and actions evidencing retention; the governing standard for supervision and
records; and dated outcomes and remediation. The first unsupported step is
the move from circular language to a particular fulfillment standard, not
the Boolean evaluation of that standard. UT Code 6.8 identifies a register
keeper; valuation-specific UT 6.11C does not set a universal tokenisation
supervision cadence. A product answer cannot turn an event log or a contract
label into a fulfillment finding without this reasoning. Source anchors:
26EC22 paragraphs 10, 11 and 19, footnote 1; UT 6.8; retained
`p10-standard.md` and `paragraph-10-dossier.md`.

## Evidence: what observations establish

An occurred reconciliation and agreement between two records are different
from accurate representation of the relevant holder's legal interest. The
23EC52 Appendix A(c)(iii) note asks separately about legal validity of
underlying ownership interests, regular reconciliation of records for
extrinsic rights with distributed-ledger tokens, and encumbrances. Paragraph
17(b) of 26EC22 calls for disclosure of the ownership representation;
Appendix footnote 1 recognizes models where the blockchain is secondary.
Accordingly, two records can agree because both copied an omitted holder,
and a mismatch with a secondary copy does not by itself determine which
record states the legal right. These are conditional counterexamples, not a
new universal record-priority rule.

Likewise, a dated supervision event does not itself show an adequate
pre-activity assessment or effective mitigation. Where 23EC52 applies to
the actor and activity, paragraph 18 requires intermediaries to understand
and be satisfied with controls before engaging in related activities;
paragraphs 23–24 connect effective mitigation of new technology risks to
the see-through complexity approach. Paragraph 9 of 26EC22 invokes only
*relevant* intermediary requirements. The source therefore supports
separating actor/activity applicability, control assessment, effective
mitigation, and P10 fulfillment. Its strongest contrary to broader use is
that the 23EC52 complexity passage does not itself specify a Product
Provider's paragraph-10 discharge rule. No source proves that a meeting
minute means controls were effective or that a loss alone changes the
product's complexity classification. Source anchors: 23EC52 paragraphs
17–18 and 23–24, Appendix A(c)(iii) and footnote 1; 26EC22 paragraphs 9,
11, 17(b), 18; retained `p10-effective-mitigation.md`.

The matched answer should state what each compared record represents, the
holder/right/time and discrepancy coverage, and the relevant standard. It
must keep a supplied premise about register primacy visible. Missing actual
rights and records bar a client-specific outcome, not this conditional
source analysis.

## P14: request, object and standard

Paragraph 14 of 26EC22 says Product Providers should *confirm* and, upon
SFC request, *demonstrate to the SFC's satisfaction* management/operational
soundness, ownership record keeping and smart-contract integrity. Footnote 5
uses “confirm and demonstrate” without repeating the request condition and
mentions, at a minimum, paragraphs 10–13 and freedom from smart-contract
vulnerabilities or security flaws with high confidence. The main text
therefore supports a request condition for demonstration, while the
footnote supplies a real attachment dispute. Reading A follows that
condition while preserving uncertainty about the footnote's content;
reading B takes the footnote's unqualified wording as an independent
standing demonstration duty; reading C uses the main-text request trigger
with footnote minimum content. B must answer the contrary express trigger;
A and C must not discard the footnote. No inspected source chooses one
as uniquely governing.

23EC52 Appendix A(b)(i) repeats high-confidence language specifically for
smart-contract audit and separately asks whether reliance on a third-party
audit is reasonable. This supports a technical-scope argument for the
phrase, but the footnote's own coordinated sentence leaves a wider reading
available. Neither a report's existence nor its confidence wording proves
that the deployed version was reviewed, that reliance was reasonable, that
paragraphs 10–13 were satisfied, or that the SFC was satisfied. Paragraph
15 separately addresses a requested third-party audit **or** verification;
paragraph 16 separately addresses a requested satisfactory legal opinion.
No paragraph-14 request does not by itself erase standing confirmation or
adjacent duties. An unknown request is not an established absence. Source
anchors: 26EC22 paragraphs 14–16 and footnote 5; 23EC52 Appendix A(b)(i);
retained `p14-attachment.md` and `paragraph-14-dossier.md`.

## Gatecoin: case proposition and transfer

The 2023 Gatecoin decision distinguishes cryptocurrency's capacity to be
trust property (paragraph 59 and conclusion 82(3)) from the existence of
an actual trust on the operative terms. Paragraphs 41–43 and 65–71 inspect
the 2018 terms, acceptance, pooled accounts and objective relationship;
paragraph 71(4) does not let an exchange-ledger “ownership” phrase displace
the other terms. That is a case-specific reason to test terms and parties
before inferring an investor's legal interest from a field label, not a
universal hierarchy of contract over every register.

The 2025 decision makes the same caution more precise for ledger events.
Paragraphs 8–9 and Annexure 1.1–1.4 treat scenarios 1–4 as not requiring
post-change platform access, subject to contrary evidence; scenarios 5–6
prima facie require such use, subject to proof of an earlier or non-platform
instruction. Paragraphs 8(3) and 9(7) reject the claimed seventh OTC
scenario for lack of factual basis, while paragraph 95(1) groups scenarios
5–7 in exception language. That textual tension is unresolved. A post-date
entry is neither conclusive acceptance nor necessarily irrelevant: event
category, actor, instruction date and contrary evidence change its weight.
The 21-day notice/evidence direction in the Annexure belongs to that case.

The 2025 Dan/Samara discussion also keeps party relationships distinct:
an obligation of Dan to Samara did not automatically create a Gatecoin-to-
Samara trust. A Product Provider transfer would require the asset/right,
governing law, constitutive terms, actual parties and capacities, accepted
terms, record role and dated later-treatment search. Gatecoin's exchange-
insolvency directions do not determine 26EC22 P10 fulfillment or a general
tokenisation rule. The strongest contrary to an overbroad transfer is that
the target may have different statutes, product rights and constitutive
records. Source anchors: 2023 paragraphs 41–43, 59, 65–71 and 82;
2025 paragraphs 8–9, 49–95 and Annexure 1.1–1.4; retained
`gatecoin-transfer.md` and `gatecoin-dossier.md`.

## What the tools must change, if anything

BM25 could help locate the Appendix note or relevant judgment paragraph,
but the retained eight-window P10 check omitted some predeclared targets;
full-source access and contrary review remain required. LegalRuleML and
restricted Logical English can preserve an explicitly supplied rival rule
or unsupported reading, but a controlled translation does not prove the
source-to-rule warrant. PyArg, Carneades and clingo can expose whether a
stated counterargument defeats a supplied argument. The retained false-
warrant control showed that a falsely labeled supported reconciliation
shortcut can pass their computation until the shared-error undercut is
supplied. The legal reviewer, not those engines, supplied the undercut.

For the eventual matched pair, record each tool's actual input and output,
which downstream request considered it, and whether the final answer
preserved or changed the source-supported distinctions above. A useful
change is one the baseline missed and the candidate supports with the
proper source and contrary, or a harmful change is an unsupported new
inference. If both arms state the same justified answer, the observed
effect is no change on that trial. No such complete original-target pair
exists yet, so all six answer-level effects remain untested. Current
authority and later treatment are not established by this local review.
