# BASF temporary-global exchange: original source review

The German paragraph §1(3)(b) on original page 108, lines 59–81, imposes
separate timing, certification and delivery requirements. Exchange cannot occur
earlier than 40 days after issue and requires certificates under U.S. tax law
about beneficial owners' non-U.S.-person status, with the stated exceptions for
certain financial institutions and certain persons holding through them.
The text does not specify the legal criteria for those excepted classes.

Interest on notes represented by the temporary global note requires prior
presentation of such certificates, separately for each payment. A certificate
received on or after day 40 is treated as a request for exchange. A request does
not prove completed exchange. The final delivery restriction begins midway
through line 79 and ends in line 81: exchanged securities may be delivered only
outside the United States, expressly importing §6(2)'s definition.

The imported definition begins at character 8 of page 124 line 68, after
`wurden.`, and ends on line 71. It includes the states, District of Columbia and
territories, with six listed territorial examples. “Including” does not make
that list exhaustive. This definition governs contractual geography; it does
not define a U.S. person or establish tax residence, citizenship or eligibility.
It lies outside the bracketed U.S.-dollar/New York and CDS agent branches, which
remain excluded. It ends before §6(3), whose complete capacity meaning remains
protected. The preceding agent-change sentence still owns `wurden.`.
