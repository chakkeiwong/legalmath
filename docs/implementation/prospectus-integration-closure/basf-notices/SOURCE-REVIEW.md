# BASF notices and proof of holding

The selected German conditions distinguish three kinds of notice. Section
13(1) requires electronic publication on the Luxembourg Stock Exchange website
and deems a notice effective on the third day after publication. Section 13(2)
keeps that rule while the notes are listed in Luxembourg, but allows the issuer
to substitute delivery to the Clearing System for forwarding to holders to the
extent the exchange rules permit. That substituted notice is deemed communicated
on the seventh day after delivery to the Clearing System. The final terms on
page 7 select both media. They do not establish a present listing or permission
under the exchange rules at a particular date.

Section 13(3) concerns a notice sent by a holder. It requires text form, such as
email or fax, or written form; delivery to the Fiscal Agent; and proof of holding
under §14(4). Delivery through the Clearing System is permitted in the manner
provided by the Fiscal Agent and that system. The issuer-publication timing is
not a general holder-notice timing rule.

Section 14(4) describes how a holder may protect or enforce rights in its own
name in proceedings against the issuer or proceedings in which both are
parties. The stated route combines a depot-bank certificate and a certified
copy of the global certificate. The bank certificate identifies the holder and
address, the aggregate nominal amount held on its date, and confirms a written
statement of those details to the Clearing System. An authorized representative
of the Clearing System or its custodian confirms the copy's correspondence
with the original; the originals need not be produced under this route. The
definition of Depotbank includes an authorized bank or other recognized custody
institution with which the holder maintains the relevant securities account,
including the Clearing System.

The last sentence expressly preserves other methods procedurally permitted in
the country of proceedings. Calling the certificate route exclusive would be
wrong relative to the inspected text. Whether another method is permitted in
an actual proceeding remains a question of applicable procedural law. This
qualification does not by itself dispense with §13(3)'s notice form and
delivery requirements.
