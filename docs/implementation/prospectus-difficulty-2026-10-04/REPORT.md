# Additional difficult prospectuses: 24-document diagnostic study

We acquired and tested **24 fresh public PDFs from 16 issuer families**: **20 issue-specific documents and four programme/base prospectuses**. The frozen reader returned **21 abstentions and three conditional positive readings**, with no runtime errors. Source review confirms a clause false positive in Mizuho's scheduled-amortization terms and missed principal-loss wording in HPB's bail-in disclosure. These cases provide concrete regression material; abstention alone is not a wrong legal answer.

All 24 PDFs, extracted pages, acquisition receipts and raw reader results are retained. The [reviewed catalogue](reviewed-catalogue.json) gives exact document dates, corrected issuers, source hashes, URLs, physical PDF page anchors and findings. Its [post-run corrections](review-errata.json) supersede mistaken descriptions in the frozen selection metadata without changing the selection or raw results.

The target is the repository's declared prospectus feature: debt with principal write-down/cancellation or compulsory common-share conversion. It includes disclosed statutory principal loss and keeps creditor-approved restructuring separate ([predicate](../../../src/legalmath/prospectus/loss_absorption.py)). It is broader than regulatory-capital triggers and narrower than every possible investment loss. Interest-only deferral, ordinary repayment and conversion of another instrument must remain distinct.

For Mizuho, the two raw witnesses are `b43d63b56e7d8c55c95ed7d3` and `b5ceca3984cffbfc9712a953`. The latter says “each Note ... shall be partially redeemed ... at the specified Amortization Amount and the outstanding principal amount ... shall be reduced”. The [amount_write_down rule](../../../src/legalmath/prospectus/loss_absorption_witnesses.py) accepts this reduction; the [paid-redemption guard](../../../src/legalmath/prospectus/reader_scope.py) is too narrow for this wording. The [reader](../../../src/legalmath/prospectus/loss_absorption_reader.py) also relies on caller-declared shelf scope to suppress these template positives. The finding concerns the claimed witnesses; it does **not** prove that every Mizuho programme option lacks loss provisions.

EDF's apparent negative control was corrected on review: the exclusion on pp. 33/136 qualifies **Exchanged Notes or Varied Notes**, not an exhaustive statement about the original perpetual notes. The useful regression is subject/scope binding, not an invented negative issue label.

The selection met the requested document range but fell short of the plan's preferred issuer diversity: 16 families overall, 13 among the issue-specific documents. Four programme tests replaced issue-level targets, some documents cover multiple series, and two issuer pairs are initial/tap versions. All remain in the denominator. The optional-conversion stratum was not independently established by this selection.

Post-run skeptical review rejected three initial interpretations: EDF as a direct negative control, proposed/future IRRD powers as already applicable, and court/limited-recourse mechanisms as categorically outside the predicate. Those corrections are reflected above and in the repair plan. The strongest alternative explanation for much of the abstention is the deliberately broad whole-document input and missing declared dependencies, rather than an inability to read every operative clause. The Mizuho paid-amortization witness remains a concrete semantic error within that input.

Only extracted PDF text was inspected; rendered-page fidelity and independent legal adjudication remain pending. Every study issue retains `source_complete=false`, `independent_adjudication=false` and no issue-level answer. No classifier tuning followed the freeze. The results establish useful engineering failures and unresolved cases, not population accuracy, complete-law correctness or production readiness.
