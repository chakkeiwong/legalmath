# What the comparative change could mean

identify the reason for the retained 122,516 movement (thousands of euros).
BES's 2009 report says that it adopted revised IAS 1 and that the effect was
presentation only. That is evidence of management's reported adoption and
reported effect. It does not say that IAS 1 caused this particular movement
between Other assets and Non-current assets held for sale. Neither an accounting
violation nor a valid accounting basis for that movement is established here.

## Distinct mechanisms and their conditions

IAS 1 originally paragraphs 27–28 and 36–41, and revised paragraphs 38–46,
address presentation and classification. Changes must meet the consistency
conditions: a required change or a more appropriate presentation after changed
operations or a review, with reliable, more relevant information and a structure
likely to continue. Comparatives are reclassified unless impracticable. The
nature, affected amounts and reason must be disclosed. If impracticable, the
reason and nature of the adjustments that would have been made are disclosed.
Impracticable means that every reasonable effort has been made; inconvenience
is not the definition. The revised paragraphs 10(f) and 39 require an opening
comparative statement of financial position for the specified retrospective
policy applications, restatements or reclassifications. These are presentation
requirements, not a cause of a particular accounting movement.

IAS 8 paragraphs 7–12 give priority to the specifically applicable standard.
The judgment hierarchy applies when none specifically applies. The original
paragraphs 7/9 refer to relevant implementation guidance and state that IASB
implementation guidance does not form part of the standards. The consolidated
paragraphs 7/9/11 have changed wording: integral guidance is mandatory;
non-integral guidance does not contain requirements. These editions must not be
described as verbatim identical. Neither version authorizes a general
comparability preference to displace a specific IFRS 5 requirement.

For an accounting-policy change, IAS 8 paragraphs 14–15 require an IFRS-mandated
change or reliable and more relevant information. Paragraph 16 excludes applying
policies to new, previously immaterial or substantively different transactions
from this category. Initial IAS 16/IAS 38 revaluations follow their own standards
(17–18). Specific transition provisions take priority (19); otherwise the
retrospective route applies, subject to practicability (22–27). That route adjusts
the opening affected equity and comparative amounts as if the policy had always
applied. Early application is not a voluntary change (20). Disclosures under
28–29 include nature, applicable transition provisions or the reason for the
voluntary change, affected line items and basic/diluted EPS if IAS 33 applies,
pre-presented-period adjustments and any impracticability circumstances and
application date. Tax effects follow IAS 12 (paragraph 4), not an inference of
zero tax consequences.

An estimate revision uses new information, developments or experience (definition
and 32–38). It is prospective in the current and, where affected, future periods;
paragraph 37 instead adjusts the relevant asset, liability or equity carrying
amount in the period of change. A changed measurement basis is a policy change;
where the distinction is difficult, paragraph 35 treats it as an estimate change.
Paragraphs 39–40 require nature and amount, with the specified exception and
disclosure when future effects cannot practicably be estimated. New information
does not, by itself, turn a past estimate into a prior-period error.

A prior-period error requires omitted or misused reliable information that was
available when those statements were authorized and could reasonably have been
obtained and considered (paragraph 5). Paragraph 41 includes presentation and
disclosure errors as well as recognition and measurement; immaterial intentional
departures to achieve a particular presentation are not a safe harbor. Material
prior errors are corrected retrospectively in the first statements authorized
after discovery, subject to 43–47. If period-specific effects cannot practicably
be determined, use the earliest practicable opening balances; if cumulative
effects cannot, correct prospectively from the earliest practicable date.
Correction is excluded from discovery-period profit or loss (46). Paragraph 49
requires the error's nature, line-item/EPS corrections, earliest opening correction
and any impracticability explanation. Paragraphs 50–53 require evidence of past
circumstances and information available at the original authorization date;
hindsight about later intentions or outcomes is prohibited. Difficulty estimating
does not automatically establish impracticability.

Accordingly IFRS 5 paragraph 40's prohibition on changing prior balance-sheet
classification merely to reflect the latest classification, already read in
attempt-007, is neither a blanket prohibition of all IAS 8 corrections nor
something the generic IAS 1 comparability rule automatically overrides. The
reason, relevant facts and period-specific authority must be established first.

## Decision and missing explanation

The primary source-reading question has a partial answer. The report supports
the adoption table and the standards support conditional, distinct mechanisms.
No inspected sentence connects the specific 122,516 property comparative to a
policy change, an identified error or a stated presentation reason. The precise
missing evidence is a transaction/line-specific reason, the affected amounts
and dates, applicable transition basis, and original-period facts. The retained
arithmetic cannot supply those facts. A further inspection needs a distinct
source lead or a qualified source explanation; repeating this note adds none.
