# Historical accounting sources and the two report policies

Four complete official English texts were obtained through twelve bounded
requests: the original Regulation 1126/2008, its 21 December 2008 and
27 December 2009 consolidations, and Regulation 1142/2009 with IFRIC 17 and its
consequential amendments. The two consolidation manifestation requests returned
HTTP 300 lists; their published DOC_1 items supplied the HTML. A direct request
for amendment work identity with XHTML content type returned HTTP 400. Its
published Cellar metadata and English expression subsequently supplied the text.
These responses and their distinct formats are retained; no unchanged request
was repeated. No challenge was solved. The twelve-request allowance is spent.

The original raw XHTML is 6,601,941 bytes, the consolidations are 3,314,084 and
3,719,934 bytes, and the amendment is 54,324 bytes. Actual bytes were streamed
and counted independently of HTTP declarations. All four transfers completed
with HTTP 200 below the eight-MiB cap. Raw bytes are retained losslessly in gzip,
with raw hashes, request/redirect/header records and exact published parent links.
This establishes the provenance of these responses, not independent authenticity

7664–7819 and December 2009 units 8322–8504. The original's five footnotes are

These 1,018 source units are retained verbatim after whitespace normalization,
with source identity, position and full raw documents available for comparison.
No illustration occurs in the relevant IFRS 5 material; no OCR or rendering is
needed. Other standards elsewhere in the downloaded compilations remain unread

The original OJ heading states publication on 29 November 2008. Article 3 says
the third day following publication, giving entry into force on 2 December 2008.
This corrects the earlier discovery note's conflation of publication with entry
into force. That historical note remains unchanged as evidence of the error.
IFRS 5 paragraph 44 separately says annual periods beginning on or after
1 January 2005; this is not a claim that Regulation 1126/2008 entered into force
in 2005. The regulation consolidates earlier EU adoptions. The entire earlier
adoption chain has not been read here.

Both consolidations explicitly describe themselves as documentation tools.
Their inclusion of an amendment is not evidence that the amendment applies to
every financial period ending by the consolidation date. Paragraph 44A requires
the IAS 1 terminology/presentation amendments for annual periods beginning on or
after 1 January 2009, with earlier application tied to earlier IAS 1 application.
The 2009 text adds 44B for IAS 27's paragraph 33(d), beginning on or after
1 July 2009, including its earlier-application and retrospective provisions.
Paragraph 44C concerns 8A and 36A: beginning on or after 1 July 2009, earlier
application only with the specified IAS 27 application and disclosure, and the
stated prospective/transitional rule. Those amendments are not the basis for
the common sale conditions in paragraphs 6–9. The separate EU amending acts for
44A–44C were identified in the consolidation but not acquired under this cap.

Regulation 1142/2009, published 27 November 2009, enters into force on
30 November by Article 3. Article 2 requires companies to apply IFRIC 17 and the
IFRS 5/IAS 10 amendments at the latest from their first financial year starting
after 31 October 2009. IFRIC 17 paragraph 18 and IFRS 5 paragraph 44D separately
state prospective annual-period application beginning on or after 1 July 2009,
prohibit retrospective application, and condition earlier application on
disclosure and applying IFRS 3, IAS 27 and IFRIC 17/the related IFRS 5 amendments.
For a calendar financial year, the EU Article 2 latest-start calculation gives
1 January 2010. Neither formulation makes every calendar-2009 report an
automatic application case. BES's actual adoption choices are not established
by this reading. The amendment adds the shareholder-approval consideration to
paragraph 8 and adds distribution-to-owners provisions; an owner distribution
is distinct from a sale of property received for a loan.

The common sale requirements are substantive. Paragraph 6 requires recovery
principally through sale. Paragraph 7 requires immediate availability in the
present condition subject only to usual and customary terms, and a highly
probable sale. Paragraph 8 requires the appropriate management commitment, an
active buyer-search/completion programme, active marketing at a price reasonable
relative to current fair value, expected completion within one year unless
paragraph 9 applies, and actions indicating significant change or withdrawal is
unlikely. Appendix A defines highly probable as significantly more likely than
probable, and probable as more likely than not; no numerical probability cut-off
is supplied by this reading.

The one-year criterion is not absolute. Paragraph 9 requires delay beyond the
entity's control and sufficient evidence of continuing commitment, in the
circumstances specified by Appendix B. B1(a) concerns expected conditions imposed
by others, actions that cannot start before a firm purchase commitment, and a
commitment highly probable within one year. B1(b) concerns unexpected buyer/other
conditions after a firm commitment, timely response and expected favourable
resolution. B1(c) concerns previously unlikely circumstances during the initial
year, timely response, continued marketing at a reasonable revised price and
continued satisfaction of paragraphs 7–8. A reported two-year banking deadline
does not itself prove any of these exceptions, and does not itself prove their
absence.

Paragraph 11 is a necessary qualification to an overly rigid acquisition-date
test: an asset acquired exclusively for subsequent disposal may qualify on
acquisition if the one-year requirement or permitted exception is met and it is
highly probable that other unmet criteria will be met shortly, usually within
three months. The three months are a usual period, not an unconditional automatic
grace period or a rigid new rule. Paragraph 12 distinguishes criteria first met
after the reporting date: it bars classification in those financial statements
while requiring specified disclosures when met before authorisation.

Measurement also needs scope. Paragraphs 2 and 5 distinguish classification and
presentation from the six measurement exclusions (tax, benefits, IAS 39
financial assets, IAS 40 fair-value property, IAS 41 fair-value assets and IFRS 4
rights). Paragraphs 18–19 require the relevant other-standard measurements.
For assets within the measurement scope, paragraphs 15–16 use the lower of
carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell, and paragraph 20 requires the
relevant write-down. Paragraphs 21–23 limit reversals and prescribe group
allocation. Paragraph 25 stops depreciation while classified held for sale;
it does not by itself justify stopping depreciation while an asset is classified
in Other assets. Paragraphs 26–29 address losing the classification and subsequent
measurement. The bank's use of the loan carrying amount on acquisition still
needs the applicable acquisition/measurement analysis; IFRS 5 alone does not
validate that amount.

Paragraph 40 adds an unresolved issue to the earlier arithmetic bridge. Prior
balance-sheet amounts are not reclassified or re-presented merely to reflect the
latest period's held-for-sale classification. Paragraph 34's re-presentation of
discontinued-operation disclosures is a different requirement. Consequently,
the change from 148,372 to 270,888 in the 2008 held-for-sale comparative needs a
specific accounting basis and explanation of its timing. The balanced 122,516
bridge does not provide that basis. This does not prove that BES's comparative
is wrong: the reason for the change and any applicable policy/error/presentation
analysis have not been established. Nor does paragraph 40 prohibit every possible
restatement for a different valid reason. Obtain the original reconciliation and
analyse the relevant IAS 1/IAS 8 provisions before deciding that issue.

All 45 retained report-policy units were reread. The 2008 report's policy 2.11
initially places loan-exchange assets in Other assets and moves them when
immediately disposable under IFRS 5; policy 2.23 states the broader sale,
availability and high-probability requirements. Its earlier non-depreciation
statement needs separate support while in Other assets. The 2009 policy 2.11
states the broad requirements and then says the Group intends immediate disposal
of all acquired property and classifies it held for sale. That policy is a
management statement, not evidence that each property satisfies every criterion.

The 2009 account of RGICSF articles 112/114, two years and written Bank of
Portugal extension remains an issuer report claim. The dated Portuguese statute
and amendment history have not been acquired. Even if that report description
were correct, a deadline and disposal intent would not imply availability,
marketing, reasonable price, timing or an Appendix B exception. Conversely,
the two-year deadline does not prove a violation, because actual earlier sale
expectations or an applicable exception may exist. Keep the conditional logic
separate from actual asset facts and from noteholder rights.
