# Statutory source analysis and conditional amendment assessment

The retained SchVG and primary 2024 amendment support a more precise conditional
analysis of the selected BASF amendment terms. They also expose an upstream
source-visibility problem for historical queries. This package is partial.
It neither completes the original law task nor establishes an effective
amendment, actual loss or an independently accepted legal answer.

The official consolidation, index and 35-page primary amending act are retained
with HTTP receipts and original bytes. SOURCE-REVIEW.md maps all 24 statutory
sections to their consequences and onward dependencies. HISTORY-REVIEW.md
distinguishes signing on 23 October 2024, publication on 29 October 2024 and the
derived 1 January 2025 commencement of Article 25. Exactly three reversed edits
to sections 9(1) and 15(2) reproduce the current extracted paragraphs when
applied forward. The older wording is a labelled reconstruction, not an
authenticated historical edition. The prior 2021 amending act is not retained.

The existing consumer discloses a creditor-amendment power separately from a
principal-loss mechanism. Its Q1 amendment list does not evaluate whether that
power has been exercised. Four matched issuer-agreement cases demonstrate this
behavior; they do not establish a Q1 implementation bug. The actual P4 and final
consumer evaluate an isolated implementation prerequisite in seven cases.
A further 14 cases evaluate 12 explicit necessary-condition families, including
scope, consent, voting eligibility, statutory and contractual majority, quorum,
procedure, publication, challenge/clearance and deed implementation. Threshold
facts are hypothetical inputs; no numerical vote or elapsed-day computation is
claimed. Satisfaction of these families is not proof of legal effectiveness.

Twelve retained historical cases pass through current consumption. The same
hypothetical unsigned text-form register fails the old signature component and
satisfies the new text-form component. The instants before and at German civil
midnight on 1 January 2025 distinguish the half-open intervals correctly.
Missing signature evidence, a rule outside its validity interval, and later
knowledge/observation remain UNKNOWN where required. The driver explicitly
selects the applicable dated basis; production does not automatically choose
an edition. Invalid intervals are rejected.

The original historical command exited 1 on its final negative case. Its
exception handler expected a P4 rejection, but P3 dependency construction first
attempted to bind a reference from an unavailable document. The recovery
verified the completed products without rerunning their computations, retained
the actual upstream error and independently confirmed that the law evaluator
rejects the unavailable source. It did not manufacture a successful P4.
The original failure remains in historical-first-execution-failure.json.

The strongest alternative explanation for the apparent integration gap is that
the request actually tries to use unavailable evidence; that should be rejected.
The next matched comparison must distinguish such use from merely retaining an
unrelated excluded source. Only the latter would establish an overbroad
construction failure. A visible active reference and an attempt to use a hidden
legal basis must remain subject to the original checks.

No production method was changed in this package. No package installation or
additional network request was needed for the diagnostics. PyMuPDF was absent
from the diagnostic interpreter; installed Poppler provided the PDF extraction
and rendering. This is environment availability evidence, not package rejection.
Three of the six permitted official GETs were used. All inputs, methods,
commands available from execution receipts, results and remaining gaps are
bound by the final manifest. Runtime/counts are descriptive engineering data.
