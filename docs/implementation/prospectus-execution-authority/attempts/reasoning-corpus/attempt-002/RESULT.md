C* = C or (D and V and P). The letter C is the cessation fact; C* names
the third candidate. A implies B because both event facts imply at least one.

The containments are strict: (C,D,V,P)=(0,1,1,1) satisfies B but not A;
(1,0,0,0) satisfies C* but not B. Their truth sets contain 1,3,9 worlds.

Across all 16 complete hypothetical worlds, the candidates all say YES only
at (1,1,1,1). They all say NO when C is false and at least one of D,V,P
is false: seven worlds. The other eight worlds disagree. A versus B differs
in two worlds, A versus C* in eight, and B versus C* in six. These distinctions
follow from the provisional formulas, not an assumed probability distribution.
Eight of sixteen is not an estimated frequency in actual transactions.

For incomplete observations, an inclusion-minimal common YES needs all four
facts true. Minimal common NO needs cessation false together with disposal
false, diminution false, or payment-risk false. Thus absence of a causally
specified consequence alone does not resolve the third candidate while cessation
remains possible. Every explicit relevant conflict is retained as CONFLICT,
even when a Boolean shortcut might determine a truth value. This is the
predeclared evidence policy, not a theorem that all legal conflicts behave so.
