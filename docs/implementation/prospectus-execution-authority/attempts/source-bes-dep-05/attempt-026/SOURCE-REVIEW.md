## Every method clause, including the cross-page continuation

The introductory methodology sentence refers instruments measured at fair
value to the financial statements for the year ended December31,2009. This
reference has not been resolved by reading Note43. It remains a named source
dependency; this attempt does not replace that methodology with an inferred
valuation hierarchy. The following paragraphs explicitly concern fair-value
estimates for instruments measured at amortised cost in the balance sheet.

For cash and deposits at central banks, deposits with banks, and loans and
advances to banks, the report calls carrying value a reasonable estimate
because of the short-term nature of the instruments. Customer loans use
discounted expected capital and interest cashflows, assuming instalments
are paid on their contractual dates. The sentence continues onto160: loans
with similar credit-risk characteristics are estimated collectively, using
current interest rates for loans with similar characteristics. Omitting the
continuation would lose both grouping and discount-rate assumptions.

Held-to-maturity investments use quoted market prices when available;
unlisted securities use discounted expected future cashflows. Deposits from
central banks and banks use the stated short-term carrying-value estimate.
Due-to-customers values use expected capital and interest at contractual
payment dates, discounted at current rates for similar instruments. The
report describes floating rates and maturity substantially below one year
as the reason its book/fair difference is not significant. This is the
report's aggregate explanation, not independent verification of every
individual instrument. Issued debt and subordinated debt use market prices
when available and discounted expected cashflows otherwise.
