The group-axis invariant follows directly from the patch: after a horizontal
join sets line_axis=1, line_is_h prevents vertical append; after a vertical join
sets line_axis=2, line_is_v prevents horizontal append. Incompatible words flush
the old group before establishing a new axis. Flushing renders with the stored
axis, independently of per-word direction bits. This prevents the inspected
retroactive-axis transition. It does not prove general reading order or correct
direction detection. The native source's separate unreachable else-if condition
is preserved in this experiment and remains an explicit limitation.

The strongest alternative explanation for successful report findings is shared
recognition and supplied reading guidance. The matched baseline, native build
reproduction, full character/source preservation and adverse controls constrain
implementation errors; they do not establish general OCR accuracy or legal truth.
A changed qualifier, unaccounted character, invalid box or changed decision field
would overturn the corresponding finite result. A real boundary-migration test
or independently reviewed direction case could overturn broader adoption claims,
which are deliberately left open. No stochastic ranking was conducted.
