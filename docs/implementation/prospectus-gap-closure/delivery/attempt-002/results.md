# Prospectus gap-closure results

The campaign covers 32 bonds: 16 qualified positives, 16 qualified negatives and 0 unresolved answers. A positive identifies a principal write-down or compulsory conversion into common shares in the selected terms. A negative is limited to the examined documents and supported constructions.

The full prospectus and bank regression suite passed 734 tests. All 32 integrated cases retained all 14 bank obligations per case. Actual customer eligibility and transaction permission remain unestablished.

## Disposition of the seven gaps

| Gap | Result | Remaining requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Monetary fields / Enel | Fixed in the bounded reader. Exact source-field and rational checks support Enel’s EUR 1,000 unit/redemption relation. | New layouts and unit conventions require additional construction tests. |
| 2. Unfamiliar legal wording | Added loss-rights/exception guards, independent money checks, tampering tests, and repaired the fresh quoted-heading defect. | Unrestricted English entailment and all possible shared interpretation errors remain unproved. |
| 3. Dependencies and editions | Quoted references and dated source selections are checked; three named Enel/Unilever source links inspected. | Unilever/Enel separate agreements, incorporated materials, amendments and the Unilever trust-deed date discrepancy remain open. |

The UBS-specific engine is connected. | Complete loss calculations for other issues, plus actual event, notice, holding and price facts. |

## Source findings

Enel’s final terms name the base prospectus of 20 December 2024 and supplement of 27 January 2025; both retained editions were checked. The final redemption amount is EUR 1,000 per EUR 1,000 Calculation Amount. The base prospectus, p.95, makes the terms subject to separate agency provisions and identifies the agency, guarantee and covenant agreements available for inspection through the agents.
Unilever’s final terms name the retained memorandum of 16 May 2025. Its summary refers to a trust deed dated 22 July 1994 (p.8), while the operative conditions identify a trust deed dated 16 May 2024 (p.32). Amendment history may explain this, but the executed agreements have not been obtained. The paying agency agreement is separately identified as 22 July 1994, as amended.
Deutsche Bank’s German terms control (p.4). The German and English columns of section 5(4), pp.52–55, both specify reduction when consolidated CET1 falls below 5.125%, pro-rata allocation and caps, prompt determination within one month unless shortened, and notice rules under which missing notice does not invalidate the reduction. Restoration is discretionary and subject to profit, distribution and continuing-trigger constraints. This local reading does not establish translation equivalence for the whole prospectus.
Lloyds’ SEC route returned HTTP403. The issuer URL returned HTTP200 with an HTML error page, not a prospectus PDF. The existing mirror remains explicitly qualified.
The first frozen challenge returned positive answers for KBC and Intesa, supported by actual write-down clauses. Inspection also found invalid supporting witnesses in a quoted heading and an uncertainty statement. Three constructed counterexamples failed before repair and passed afterward. The original frozen result is preserved; both cases are now exposed regression evidence.
The acquisition ledger records all 40 retrieval attempts, including failures, discovery pages, two rejected PDF candidates and source-selection decisions. No corporate issue bundle was obtained. Source availability is therefore a material limit on the challenge, and there is no population-accuracy claim.
