From the circular to executable
controls
A circular supplies words; a Java program needs precise decisions. Between them lie
questions about meaning, evidence and execution. The eight steps below locate that
work. Each numbered box links to a closer view on the next pages, where the technol-
ogy is introduced through the job it performs.
Several names in the map refer to different layers of that route. The shared frontend
retains the source, scope, reviewed interpretation and evidence policy, then records
them in a typed LegalRuleModel. The model is a record of what a proposed control
means and which evidence it expects; it is not legal authority. RuleIR is the project’s
narrower executable rule language. Catala is a separate executable target with its own
compiler and exception semantics. The completed implementation can lower the com-
mon scalar profile to Catala and can also emit native Catala directly from richer shared
models. Stipula belongs to a different comparison: it describes stateful contracts with
parties, actions and transitions, and is discussed as a research route rather than as a
LegalMath backend. Chapters 4 and 8 return to these distinctions with examples and
test evidence.
