6.34.1 The objects that travel through the investigation
The dossier starts with a source packet. The packet identifies the oﬀicial document bytes,
edition, selected passages, cited dependencies and the date and scope of the question.
A digest of the packet is part of every later record. A candidate that quotes the same
sentence from a different edition is consequently a different candidate, even if the vis-
ible sentence has not changed. This is the same discipline used by the authority and
temporal records described earlier in this chapter.
The next object is a set of interpretation hypotheses. A hypothesis has an identifier, an
interpretation family, the source passages on which it depends, controlled English, as-
sumptions, supporting and opposing reasons, and either a RuleIR bundle or an explicit
statement that it cannot yet be encoded. The family records a choice that changes legal
consequences: for example, a later refund may be treated as a fee discount, or the ex-
ception may be attached only to the fee component of a combined offer. Repeating the
same prompt with different wording does not create a new family.
An argument record links those hypotheses to premises, exceptions, counterarguments
and source evidence. ASPIC+ distinguishes attacks on a premise, an inference and a
conclusion; Carneades distinguishes ordinary premises, assumptions and exceptions
(Prakken, 2010, secs. 2–3) (Gordon et al., 2007, secs. 3–6). LegalMath borrows these
distinctions in its bounded graph. The graph can show that a reading is supported, op-
posed or blocked under the recorded premises. It does not turn an unresolved priority
between two legal reasons into a Boolean answer.
A method result says what one assurance route did. It contains a method family, tar-
get question, input hash, implementation hash, result hash, shared dependencies, sta-
tus and limitations. The result also names retained files: the exact request, response,
checker output, compiled program and replay cases where applicable. The verifier
reads those files and recomputes their hashes. A hash of a sentence saying “the check
passed” is insuﬀicient evidence. The status vocabulary includes AGREES, DISAGREES,
UNRESOLVED, UNAVAILABLE, NOT_COMPARABLE and STALE. The validator requires at least
two method families for a required comparison. It also records shared dependencies:
generated Java and the optional Catala pilot share RuleIR and fact preparation, so their
agreement is a translation check, not two independent interpretations.
Finally, a proof obligation states a formal proposition, assumptions, checker and domain.
It records one of PROVED, CHECKED, DISPROVED, UNKNOWN, UNSUPPORTED or NOT_RUN. A
proved obligation must carry a certificate accepted by a registered proof checker;
this integration currently has no registered proof-certificate adapter.
An unknown
or unsupported obligation must carry a limitation. For example, a finite exhaustive
replay can establish that a generated Java class agrees with a Python evaluator on
the listed snapshots. It cannot establish that the English phrase “discount of fees or
charges” includes every future rebate.
The objects are deliberately separate because they answer different questions. The fol-
lowing table is the implementation boundary.
