\chapter*{The decision this book supports}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The decision this book supports}
Senior management is being asked to consider a narrow question: can a bank turn
a regulatory circular into a repeatable client or product decision without
losing the meaning of the source along the way? The answer matters because a
program can apply a mistaken interpretation perfectly. The damaging error is
often made before implementation, when an exception is missed, the wrong person
is assessed, or a requirement is applied to a different activity.

This book presents the work completed so far and the boundary that remains. It
does not present an autonomous legal decision maker. It presents a research
prototype and a design for a supervised bank control. The prototype keeps the
source passage, the proposed reading, the evidence supporting each fact, the
resulting rule, and the review decision together. A later reader can therefore
ask not only ``What did the control return?'' but also ``Which requirement did
it apply, to whom, at what time, and on what evidence?''

\section*{What has been done}

The work follows two public SFC circulars through the same chain of questions.
The first concerns the streamlined treatment of sophisticated professional
investors. A synthetic client is followed through financial qualification,
product category, exposure, consent, continuing duties and a proposed
transaction. The second concerns incentives used when marketing authorised
funds. It shows why a fee discount may fall within an exception while a voucher
in the same offer may require a separate assessment. These examples are
deliberately ordinary. Their value is that a small change in a fact, a date, or
the subject being assessed changes the responsible answer.

The prototype has turned those examples into explicit rules, generated Java
behaviour, boundary cases, amendment and withdrawal scenarios, and a review
record that can be replayed. The engineering demonstrations cover the route from
an adopted interpretation to a tested release candidate and back to an earlier
decision when the facts or the rule version changes. This is evidence that the
chosen controls can be executed and inspected. It is not evidence that the
underlying legal interpretation is correct for every product or client.

\section*{The contribution}

The contribution is a disciplined middle layer between a circular and a bank
system. It gives compliance and legal reviewers a place to state what a passage
means, what remains uncertain, and which choice belongs to bank policy. It gives
engineering a precise specification to implement. It gives operations a result
that includes the evidence request or human review route when the available
information cannot support a safe conclusion.

Four distinctions carry most of the value. The work separates a source duty
from an additional bank restriction. It keeps an unknown fact different from a
false fact. It binds a fact to the person, product, component, date and activity
to which it belongs. It also preserves the knowledge available at the time of a
past decision, so a later correction can explain the history rather than silently
rewrite it. These distinctions are easy to state and easy to lose in a compact
flag or a large language model prompt.

The method also treats disagreement as information. A second reading that finds
an omitted paragraph, an exception, or a different subject of the rule creates a
specific review question. Agreement among several readings is useful evidence
within the examined scope, but it is not a legal vote and it cannot reveal a
requirement that every reading omitted.

\section*{What the evidence supports today}

The retained demonstrations show that the proposed chain can preserve source
references, compare competing cases, evaluate explicit rules, detect selected
implementation mutations, and carry a decision through withdrawal, amendment,
release and historical replay. The examples use public documents and synthetic
facts. They establish the behaviour of the demonstration under its stated
assumptions.

Several decisions remain outside that evidence. The bank has not yet supplied
an approved legal interpretation for a live business perimeter, independently
validated the meanings and data mappings, measured performance against a
reference set of real cases, or connected the result to production identity,
confidentiality, audit and operational controls. The product therefore cannot
approve a client, clear a marketing offer, or replace a qualified reviewer.

\section*{The management decision}

The sensible next step is a narrow, supervised prototype with a named legal
owner and an agreed set of products, entities, dates and evidence sources. Before
any production use, the bank would need independent legal validation, a review
of the data and identity mappings, security and audit controls, an evaluation on
representative cases, and a shadow period in which the system's results are
compared with decisions made by qualified staff. A failed or unresolved case
must remain visible and route to a human decision.

The remainder of the book explains how to make that decision responsibly. It
begins with the two circulars and the concrete mistakes they expose. It then
introduces formal meaning, competing interpretations, execution checks,
evaluation and operating controls at the point each is needed. The detailed
implementation record is retained for readers who must build or audit the
system, but the argument begins with the banking decision rather than with the
history of the build.
