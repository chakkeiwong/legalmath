\section*{The proposed investment}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{The proposed investment}
Private-bank regulatory change repeatedly crosses a difficult boundary: a circular
describes what the institution should do, while software requires exact inputs,
conditions, dates and consequences. Much of the interpretation occurs in documents,
meetings and tickets that are only loosely connected to the eventual code. A small
change to an exception, client category or monitoring event can therefore survive
ordinary testing while changing the treatment of clients.

LegalMath would make that boundary a product. Its central deliverable is a
\emph{reviewable regulatory specification}: the relevant source passages, their
interpretation, typed rules, unresolved questions, examples, and the evidence
supporting execution. Compliance officers review the meaning in ordinary language
and worked cases; engineers implement or integrate the same versioned rules; tests
and formal checks report precisely which properties hold. A flowchart is one view
of this specification, alongside decision tables and event histories.

The literature supports a credible route. Catala provides a model for executable
statutes with explicit exceptions and close source alignment. Stipula provides
contract states, permissions and timed interactions. LegalRuleML and eFLINT explain
why legal authority, duties and interpretations need their own representation.
Recent neurosymbolic work supports using language models to propose formalizations
and using symbolic tools to evaluate them. It also shows that translation errors
remain the main obstacle: a proof can establish a property of the formal rules
without establishing that those rules capture the circular.

This proposal recommends a staged prototype centred on the SFC--HKMA sophisticated
professional investor (SPI) circular and its annexes, with separate amendment and
marketing cases. Start with reviewed rules, a small interpreter, deterministic
explanations and independent test scenarios. Add language-model drafting and
counterexample search once that baseline works. Compare Catala and decision-table
adapters on the same cases; evaluate a Stipula-inspired event model separately.
Existing MathDevMCP, DynareMCP and ResearchAssistant components can support proof
evidence, investigative search and source management through narrow adapters.

The initial commercial decision is whether to fund a limited discovery and
prototype exercise.
The exercise should measure review effort, materially wrong conclusions, missed
obligations and amendment handling against the existing manual process. The
proposed schedule is twelve weeks with named compliance participation. Timing and
staffing are planning assumptions; no savings or accuracy result has yet been
measured. The study includes 25 downloaded paper editions representing 24 works,
all seven requested sources, and additional standards and software documentation.
